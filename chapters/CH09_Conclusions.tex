\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{ch:conclusions}

\section{Summary of the Work}
\label{sec:conclusions-summary}

This thesis asked whether a liquid neural network can serve as the
\emph{microbrain} of a hardware-independent platform --- consuming an
observation whose form does not depend on the sensor that produced it, and
commanding vehicles whose kinematics it reads from that same observation ---
and which of its variants is best suited to the task. To answer it, three
continuous-time cells, LTC, CfC and LRCU, were implemented, each with a dense
wiring and with a sparse one following the Neural Circuit Policies, around a
sparse convolutional encoder that reduces the voxel cloud to a descriptor; the
six networks, plus a control identical to them but without state, were trained
in two stages --- imitation of a corpus of recorded flights and fine-tuning by
reinforcement on a fleet of parallel simulations --- and compared with
everything that is not the cell and the wiring held equal.

\paragraph{The first part of the question has a positive answer.}
The whole chain works, and it was verified one level at a time. The three cells
learn a known dynamics, with the CfC networks within three times the noise
floor (Section~\ref{sec:res-sl-synthetic}); the recurrent agent is correct and
learns on a reference problem with every cell (Section~\ref{sec:res-rl-lunar});
the imitation of the corpus generalises to unseen episodes, with a test error
about a third of that of the predictor echoing the observed velocity and the
same ranking as on validation (Table~\ref{tab:res-test}); and the imitated
command uses the map, since removing its content degrades the loss by up to
22\% (Table~\ref{tab:res-encoder}). On the fleet, finally, fine-tuning takes the
share of episodes that reach the goal from below 10\% to between 36\% and 63\%,
on a mixed fleet of multirotors and rovers that a single policy commands by
reading their limits from the observation (Section~\ref{sec:res-rl-fleet}). A
liquid network can therefore serve as the microbrain of this platform, and the
two-stage procedure produces a policy that flies a task imitation alone does
not.

\paragraph{The second part has no winner.}
None of the three stages singles out one cell as better than the others in an
established way. The networks split each time into two groups --- the CfC
networks and the control on one side, LTC and LRCU on the other --- and the
separation between the groups exceeds the variability within the runs, while
the differences within each group do not. Above all, in all three stages the
stateless control is among the best: second by loss on the corpus, the fastest
on LunarLander, level with the best recurrent network on the fleet
(Section~\ref{sec:res-discussion}). On the tasks used, then, memory has not
shown that it is worth its state, and the hypothesis that motivated the choice
of liquid networks --- that a memory consistent with physical time helps
navigation --- remains neither confirmed nor refuted.

If a candidate must nevertheless be named, the measurements point to the CfC
cell, for practical reasons more than for accuracy. It converges before the
others: below the threshold of the sine task after a single epoch, and at its
minimum on the corpus after eleven, while LTC and LRCU are still descending
after twenty. It is the only one of the three cells that receives the real
interval as an argument rather than as a component of the observation. And in
its dense variant it is the recurrent network with the highest arrival rate on
the fleet. LRCU is the variant to advise against in its current form: its state
grows with the length of the episode --- up to norms of $300$--$390$ on
LunarLander and $72$--$77$ on the fleet, against less than $7$ for the others
--- and on LunarLander it coincides with the only disastrous episodes at the
end of training.

\paragraph{The contributions, in the light of the results.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{The observation contract.} A single policy commands multirotors
        and rovers on the same fleet, reading their limits from the
        observation, with no special case in the loss or in the reward. The
        result concerns two platforms and one grid, and is not to be extended
        beyond what the declaration of the vehicle describes.
  \item \emph{The controlled comparison.} The three cells and the two wirings
        were put on the same bench with everything else fixed, in three stages,
        beside a control without state. The control is what made the outcome
        readable: without it, the ranking of the cells would have been reported
        as a ranking of memories.
  \item \emph{The two-stage training and its instrumentation.} The metrics did
        the job they were introduced for. The skill against echoing the velocity
        showed that the high $R^2$ on yaw was due to the persistence of the
        signal; the restart ratio identified the main defect of the imitation
        before the fleet confirmed it; the end reasons and the progress, and not
        the return, described what fine-tuning actually taught.
  \item \emph{The negative results.} Two limits stated in
        Chapter~\ref{ch:introduction} were measured. Imitation teaches the
        encoder the geometry of the scene only in part, and least on the fleet's
        own grid (Table~\ref{tab:res-encoder}); and a satisfied budget
        constraint is not enough to prevent the policy from learning to lose the
        vehicle or not to advance, as the preliminary sessions show
        (Section~\ref{sec:res-rl-fleet}). A third, unforeseen, adds to them: the
        imitation loss does not predict how the imitated policy flies on the
        fleet ($\rho = -0.14$, Section~\ref{sec:res-cross}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What the fleet showed about imitation.}
The link between the two stages is the result that most orients future work.
The corpus showed policies that at departure command 2\% of what the pilot
commanded; the fleet showed imitated policies that run out of time in most
episodes; fine-tuning corrected above all that. And among the quantities
measured on the imitation, the one that best predicts the final result is not
the loss but the dependence of the command on the map ($\rho = +0.79$,
Section~\ref{sec:res-cross}). The imitation stage should therefore be judged,
and selected, on its use of the map and not on its mean error alone.


\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusions-future}

The future developments follow directly from the limits stated in
Section~\ref{sec:res-discussion}, and are ordered from those that would
strengthen the conclusions of this thesis to those that would extend them.

\paragraph{Making the comparison statistically sound.}
Each network was trained once per stage. Repeating at least the sessions of the
two groups with more seeds is the condition for turning indicative differences
into established ones, and it is the first thing to do. Two changes to the
fleet's logging go in the same direction: arrival rates and end reasons per
slice, so as to separate multirotors and rovers and the four levels of
difficulty, and a step budget large enough for every network to reach a
plateau, which on the corpus and on the fleet was not always reached.

\paragraph{Correcting the imitation where it fails.}
The main defect of the supervised stage is the departure, which a mean squared
loss on an ambiguous demonstration pushes towards nothing. Three routes, in
order of cost: weighting the restart frames more heavily in the loss; selecting
the checkpoint also on the dependence of the command on the map, which on the
fleet proved more predictive than the loss; replacing the
deterministic head with one that represents several modes, so that ``hold
still'' and ``depart'' are not averaged. To these adds a larger and more
balanced corpus, containing the lateral component that is absent today.

\paragraph{An encoder that represents the obstacles.}
The probe shows that the directional distance to the obstacles is the part of
the scene the encoder represents worst, precisely on the fleet's grid. The
auxiliary geometry term is already in the code and was switched off during
fine-tuning; measuring its effect on the fleet, and on the one grid the fleet
uses, is a direct experiment.

\paragraph{Separating the wiring from the skip connections.}
The advantage of the NCP wiring on the corpus cannot be attributed to its
sparsity rather than to the two skip connections that come with it. A dense
variant with the same skip connections would separate the two.

\paragraph{From the simulator to the robot.}
No policy has yet been run on a real robot. The observation pipeline is the same
on board and in simulation, and the path exporting a checkpoint to the
inference node exists; the next step is to measure on the real vehicle how much
of the arrival rate obtained in simulation survives dynamics, sensors and
latencies that are not simulated.
